# Robotiq 2F-85 gripper

The [Robotiq 2F-85](https://robotiq.com/products/adaptive-grippers) is a two-finger adaptive
gripper with an 85 mm stroke. It sits on the Gen3's wrist and is driven through the arm's
interconnect, so it has no cable, network address or driver of its own. Every command goes
through [kinova-gen3-ros2](/kinova-gen3-ros2).

This page covers how a command reaches the fingers, what the gripper reports, how it moves, and
how to command it to move smoothly at a speed you choose.

<Callout>
  The numbers below were measured on the Dojo cell on 2026-10-29, with the gripper only and no
  arm motion, through the `kinova-gen3-ros2` nightly. The tool, raw data and full report are in
  [rammp-kitchen-capability-engines](https://github.com/rammp-org/rammp-kitchen-capability-engines)
  under `scripts/gripper_char/` and `docs/gripper-characterization.md`. Another gripper unit may
  differ slightly: re-measure yours with the same tool before relying on a timing-critical
  constant. Distances in mm are position × the 85 mm stroke.
</Callout>

## How a command reaches the fingers

You publish `/setpoint/gripper` (`rammp_arm_interfaces/GripperSetpoint`). Each field is 0..1:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `position` | 0 is open, 1 is closed. |
| `speed` | The finger speed. See [Speed](#speed): 0 does **not** move the fingers. |
| `force` | A ceiling on the motor current, not a force setpoint. |
| `token` | The control token. The arbiter checks it when arbitration is enabled. |

The driver clamps each field to 0..1 and stamps it, as a percentage, into every frame of the
arm's 1 kHz BaseCyclic command, in the interconnect gripper's `motor_cmd`. The gripper
**latches the last command**: once you stop publishing, it keeps the last position, speed and
force. The setpoint is accepted during goals and streams, so you can move the fingers while the
arm moves.

## What it reports

| Topic | Rate | Carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/gripper_state` | 20 Hz | `position` (0..1), `effort` (current / max current, not newtons), `current` (A), `present` |
| `/joint_states`, `*_robotiq_85_left_knuckle_joint` | ~50 Hz | position × 0.8 rad. Velocity and effort are NaN. |

The knuckle in `/joint_states` is the fastest position signal available. Its velocity is NaN
because the driver drops the motor's velocity from the arm's feedback.

<Callout type="warning">
  About 1 sample in 3,000 of `/gripper_state`, and 1 in 10,000 of the knuckle, reads exactly
  `0.0` in the middle of a stroke. Don't act on one sample alone.
</Callout>

## How it moves

The velocity profile of a move is a trapezoid with nearly vertical edges. The fingers start about 50 ms after the
setpoint, run at the commanded speed, and stop dead on the setpoint with no overshoot.

### Speed

For any `speed` > 0:

```
finger speed ≈ 20.9 + 128.4 × speed   mm/s     (speed ≤ 0.75; ±0.5 mm/s)
```

![Commanded speed against measured finger speed](/gripper-2f85-speed.png)

| speed | 0 | 0.005 | 0.01 | 0.1 | 0.2 | 0.3 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 1.0 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| mm/s | **0** | 21.8 | 22.2 | 33.7 | 46.6 | 58.9 | 84.1 | 118 | 161 closing, 147 opening |

- **There is a floor, not a dead band.** `speed` 0 does not move the fingers at all, and any
  `speed` above 0 moves them at about 21 mm/s or faster. The 2F-85 cannot move continuously
  slower than that.
- **Closing and opening run at the same speed** up to 0.75. Above that, closing is faster.
- **`force` has no effect on free-air speed.**
- **Repeats agree to ±0.1 mm/s.**

### Latency

From publishing a setpoint to the fingers moving: **42–55 ms median**, p90 under 60 ms, worst
case 68 ms. Closing is about 7 ms quicker than opening. The `speed` setting has little effect.

### Position

- **Accuracy:** after a move, the reported position equals the command to within one step of
  1/255 (0.3 mm).
- **Repeatability:** repeats show no spread, and approaching from either direction lands in the
  same place.
- **Full close:** in free air, a full close reads 0.991.
- **Gap in mm:** the mapping from position to the physical gap between the fingers has not been
  measured.

### Restarting

Once the fingers have stopped, they wait about **0.1 s** before following a setpoint that has
moved on. This causes the stair-steps you see when you stream a position ramp with a `speed`
faster than the ramp itself: the fingers catch the setpoint, stop, wait, and jump. Each step is
about the ramp's rate × 0.1 s.

### Contact

On an object the fingers stop dead, so position is the signal to watch. Once they hold, the
current drops to about 0.05 A whatever the `force`, so current tells you nothing about a stall at
20 Hz. At `force` 1, though, the fingers keep creeping for up to 0.6 s as they squeeze.

Detecting the stall from position:

- two unchanged `/gripper_state` samples: **0.10–0.14 s** after the stop;
- three unchanged knuckle samples: **0.06 s** after the stop.

## Commanding it smoothly

To reach `target` in `T` seconds without stair-steps:

- **The rate:** `rate = |target − current| × 85 mm / T`.
- **At 21 mm/s or faster:** send `target` itself at
  `speed = clamp((rate − 20.9) / 128.4, 0.01, 1)`. The fingers make one continuous move and
  arrive about 50 ms after `T`. Do **not** stream a position ramp with a higher `speed`: the
  fingers keep catching up to it and stair-step.
- **Slower than 21 mm/s:** the fingers cannot go that slowly. Stream the position ramp at 50 Hz
  with `speed` 0.01. They follow it in 0.3 mm steps, which looks continuous, and arrive on time.
- **A step:** send the target at `speed` 1.
- **Settling:** the fingers are done once they are within 0.01 of the target. If they stop short
  (on an object), they are done once they have been still for 0.1 s (two `/gripper_state`
  samples). Don't judge stillness until about 0.15 s after the command, to cover the latency.

In a capability, `self.arm.gripper_ramp(position, by=T)` does this, and `self.arm.gripper()`
settles the same way. The constants are `GRIPPER_V0_M_S`, `GRIPPER_K_M_S`, `GRIPPER_MIN_SPEED`,
`GRIPPER_ARRIVED`, `GRIPPER_JUDGE_AFTER_S` and `GRIPPER_STILL_S` in
`capability_engine/capability_engine/arm.py`.

On the arm, the gripper moved 0 → 0.8 while a cuRobo trajectory moved the tool 15 cm in 2.1 s.
One setpoint at the computed speed finished 3–17 ms after the arm, 0.16 mm off a straight line.
The old approach, a position ramp streamed at 3× the needed speed, finished 60–120 ms late in
3–4 mm steps.

![Gripper moving 0 → 0.8 during a cuRobo trajectory, closing on the way out (top) and opening on the way back (bottom): one setpoint at the computed speed (left, right) against a streamed ramp (middle)](/gripper-2f85-demo.png)

The gripper moves at a constant speed while the arm follows cuRobo's smooth profile. They start
and end together, but in between they are up to about 15 % of the move apart.

## Re-measuring

The tool runs on a cell through `hil`, in the driver's own image. It publishes only
`/setpoint/gripper`, refuses to start if anything else does, and returns the fingers to where
they started. From rammp-kitchen-capability-engines:

```bash
scripts/gripper_char/run.sh probe                                  # read-only: rates, position
scripts/gripper_char/run.sh run speed --reps 2 --out /data/<dir>   # moves the fingers
scripts/gripper_char/run.sh demo_trajectory.py --dry-run           # plans only; without it, moves the arm
```

The plans are `speed`, `force`, `position`, `latency`, `ramps` and `contact`. The `contact` plan
needs an object in the jaws. `analyze.py` turns a run's raw data into metrics and plots.
