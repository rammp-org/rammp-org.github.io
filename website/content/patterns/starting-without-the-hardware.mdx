# Starting without the hardware

Sheppy brings every node up at once. The arm takes a minute to boot, the
RealSense re-enumerates after the arm's USB hub, and the LUCI chair goes to
sleep on its own and wakes when someone touches it. A node that needs one of
those cannot assume it is there when the node starts, or that it stays there.

The drink node used to raise after a short wait for the wrist camera, so it
died whenever it was launched before the camera. The LUCI gRPC node still exits
when the chair does not answer within its connection timeout, and since sheppy
does not respawn a node that exits, the chair stays undriveable until someone
notices and restarts it by hand.

## The convention

**Launch order never matters.** A node starts, tells you what it is waiting for,
and keeps waiting.

- **Wait without a deadline, and say so on a schedule.** Log once that the peer
  is missing, then a reminder every 10 s or so with the topic or address you
  are waiting on. The drink node waits for `/camera/wrist/color/image_raw` and
  logs "Is the wrist RealSense running?" every 10 s until it arrives.
- **Retry on a timer that cancels itself.** The arm driver tries to connect to
  the Kortex arm at 2 Hz from a timer created in `__init__`, and cancels the
  timer the first time it succeeds. The node is up, its services exist, and
  the rest of the system can discover it in the meantime.
- **Serve what you can while you wait.** Publish your status topic at 1 Hz from
  the start so the system can show "arm: not connected" rather than "arm:
  missing". Answer commands with a clear rejection ("arm not connected") rather
  than a timeout.
- **Treat losing the peer like never having had it.** The arm driver's comms
  watchdog moves to `ERROR` after 0.5 s without feedback, and a `clear_error`
  service brings it back once the arm answers again. The operator does not
  restart the node.
- **Never exit because a peer is absent.** An exit code is a message to a
  human, and on the chair no human is watching the log.

<Callout type="warning">
  Do not confuse this with tolerating a *broken* peer. A camera that publishes
  garbage or an arm that reports a fault is an error to report loudly, not a
  peer to wait for. The distinction is whether the operator can fix it by
  turning something on.
</Callout>

## What the system does with it

`system_control` decides the chair is ready only when every expected node is
present and the base's diagnostics report LUCI and the Teensy connected. While
something is missing it logs the list every 5 s, and the GUI shows the system
as initialising. That is what your node's waiting state feeds into: the
operator sees one summary, not a stack trace in one container's log.

## What does not belong here

- **A long `time.sleep()` at startup** to let the hardware come up. It is a
  guess, it is wrong on the day the camera takes longer, and it delays the
  nodes that were ready.
- **Retrying inside a callback.** See
  [Waiting without spinning](/patterns/waiting-without-spinning).
- **Depending on sheppy to respawn you.** It does not.

## A module that does this

The arm driver in Demo-Software (`hardware/arm_driver`): a 2 Hz connect timer
that cancels itself, a 1 Hz status topic from the first second, commands
rejected with "not connected" until the arm answers, and a watchdog that turns
a lost connection into a recoverable `ERROR` state.
