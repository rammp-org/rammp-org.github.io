# Perception compute budget

Perception shares the chair's Jetson AGX Orin with the arm driver, the
planner and every module. This page fixes the rates the perception stack
runs at, gives what it costs at those rates, and records how the options
compare. The measurements are from October 2026, on the `sha-8091bbee`
perception images. The tooling to repeat them is in
[rammp-deployments `perf/`](https://github.com/rammp-org/rammp-deployments/tree/dev/perf).

## The stack and its rates

| Node | Runs | Rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `scene_camera` | Orbbec Gemini 336L, 1280 × 720 color and registered depth | 30 fps |
| `scene_segmenter` | YOLOE-26m-seg on the GPU (TensorRT FP16), BoT-SORT tracking | 10 Hz: `frame_stride: 3` |
| `sam3` | SAM3: expert detection on request, tracking sessions | tracking at most 5 Hz: `max_tracking_rate_hz: 5.0` |
| `object_lifter` | BoxerNet 3D boxes for the segmenter's objects | at most 5 Hz: `max_rate_hz: 5.0` |

Every perception container also runs with `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1`. Without
it, numpy's thread pool keeps up to five CPU cores busy per detector (see
[CPU](#cpu)).

At these rates, with SAM3 tracking one object, the whole stack costs this on
abra:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Always-on detections | 10 Hz, 116 ms from camera capture to result (median) |
| SAM3 tracking | 4.7 Hz, 183 ms |
| BoxerNet 3D boxes | 5.0 Hz, 281 ms |
| GPU busy | 61% |
| CPU | 27% of 12 cores |
| Memory | about 15 GB (measured on snorlax, see [Memory](#memory)) |
| Module power | 39 W |

GPU busy is the fraction of time the GPU was running anything (`GR3D_FREQ` in
`tegrastats`). Power is the sum of the three monitored rails. Latency is
receive time minus the camera frame's stamp, so it includes the camera driver
(about 50 ms on the Gemini).

<Callout type="info">
The stack keeps its rates when something else loads the GPU. Next to a
process keeping the GPU busy 70% of the time, the detector ran at 9.9 Hz, SAM3
tracking at 4.5 Hz and BoxerNet at 5.0 Hz. See [Sharing the GPU](#sharing-the-gpu).
</Callout>

## Where the GPU goes

Each node's share is the difference between runs with and without it, at the
rates above.

| Node | GPU busy |
| --- | --- |
| SAM3 tracking one object at 5 Hz | 24% (48% uncapped, at about 12 Hz) |
| BoxerNet at 5 Hz | about 30% (about 40% lifting every detection, at 8.7 Hz) |
| YOLOE-26m-seg at 10 Hz | about 10% |
| Scene camera, planner (idle), foxglove | 0% |

A SAM3 detect call (four prompts, 0.6–1.2 s depending on load) holds the GPU for its whole
duration. A tracking session waits for it, so tracking pauses for about a
second during each call.

## Choosing the always-on detector

The always-on detector has to report the Orchestrator's object classes (food
container, drink cup, fridge, microwave, table), with masks, at 10 Hz. SAM3
covers the hard cases on request. Each row below is the detector at 10 Hz,
next to SAM3 tracking at 5 Hz, with the OpenBLAS fix.

| Detector | Rate | Camera to result | Its GPU share | Classes | Masks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **YOLOE-26m-seg, GPU** | 10.0 Hz | 98 ms | ~10% | all five (open vocabulary) | yes |
| YOLOE-26s-seg, GPU | 10.0 Hz | 91 ms | ~6% | all five | yes |
| YOLOE-26s-seg, DLA0 | **9.3 Hz** | 175 ms | ~8% | all five | yes |
| RF-DETR-Seg S, GPU | 10.0 Hz | 100 ms | ~18% | COCO only: food container only as "bowl" | yes |
| NanoOWL B/32, GPU | 10.0 Hz | 66 ms | ~9% | open vocabulary | **no**, boxes |
| OWLv2 B/16, GPU | **1.5 Hz** | 744 ms | the whole GPU | open vocabulary | **no**, boxes |

- **YOLOE-26m on the GPU** is the always-on detector. It has the best YOLOE
  masks, covers every class, and costs little GPU at 10 Hz.
- **YOLOE-26s** is cheaper but has weaker masks. In the 2026-10-08 down-select
  it missed close-up microwave views that 26m caught.
- **The DLA** frees most of the GPU, but a frame takes 94 ms there, so it
  cannot hold 10 Hz in the stack: 9.3 Hz alone, 7.7 Hz with BoxerNet, 7.2 Hz
  next to a busy GPU (the detection head falls back to the GPU). It is worth
  revisiting with an INT8 engine if the GPU becomes the limit.
- **RF-DETR-Seg** gives the cleanest masks and holds 10 Hz in the full stack
  for a little more GPU (about 18% against 10%), but knows only COCO classes.
- **NanoOWL** and **OWLv2** return boxes only. OWLv2 also takes the whole GPU.

Mask and class quality are from the detector down-select of 2026-10-08 on abra,
with SAM3 as pseudo ground truth. This page covers only cost.

## Measurements

### Setup

- **abra**: AGX Orin 64 GB devkit, L4T r36.4.7, MAXN with clocks locked,
  PREEMPT_RT. The Gemini 336L was the scene camera.
- **snorlax**: the December demo machine. A Seeed AGX Orin 64 GB, L4T r36.4.4,
  MAXN with clocks locked, stock kernel. Its cameras were not cabled yet, so it
  replayed 60 frames recorded from abra's Gemini, at 30 fps with fresh stamps.
  Latencies there leave out the camera driver.
- Every node ran as the december_2026 manifest runs it. The rest of the stack
  ran next to it: scene camera, cuRobo planner (idle), foxglove bridge, and the
  arm and its TF as mocks, because abra's arm was off the network. There was no
  wrist camera, so its driver's CPU is not counted.
- Each run waited for every topic, warmed up for 15 s, then measured for 60 s.
- SAM3 tracked one object ("microwave"). Five detect calls with four prompts
  each were made during each measured minute.

### Each detector at full rate

Every camera frame offered (`frame_stride: 1`), with SAM3 loaded and idle.
These runs were before the OpenBLAS fix, which is why CPU is so high.

| Detector | Rate | Node ms per frame | Camera to result | GPU busy | Detector CPU |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| YOLOE-26m-seg, GPU | 18.2 Hz | 52 | 130 ms | 15% | 7.7 cores |
| YOLOE-26s-seg, GPU | 19.1 Hz | 44 | 128 ms | 9% | 7.7 cores |
| YOLOE-26s-seg, DLA0 | 9.8 Hz | 100 | 166 ms | 1% | 4.9 cores |
| RF-DETR-Seg S, GPU | 23.1 Hz | 42 | 115 ms | 35% | 4.0 cores |
| NanoOWL B/32, GPU | 30.0 Hz (camera rate) | 14 | 65 ms | 24% | 0.5 cores |
| OWLv2 B/16, GPU | 2.5 Hz | 392 | 458 ms | 97% | 0.2 cores |

With BoT-SORT's default camera-motion compensation (`gmc_method:
sparseOptFlow`), YOLOE spends about 12 ms per frame of CPU on optical flow.
`gmc_method: none` saves that if ids hold without it.

### Sharing the GPU

A synthetic load (`perf/gpu_load.py`, FP16 matrix multiplies in 20 ms bursts)
stood in for the other GPU users on the chair: the planner and modules.

| Configuration | Detector | SAM3 tracking | BoxerNet | GPU busy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Finalized stack | 10.0 Hz, 116 ms | 4.7 Hz | 5.0 Hz | 61% |
| Finalized stack, other user busy 70% | 9.9 Hz, 148 ms | 4.5 Hz | 5.0 Hz | 91% |
| No BoxerNet, other user busy 40% | 10.0 Hz, 111 ms | 4.7 Hz | – | 64% |
| No BoxerNet, other user busy 70% | 10.0 Hz, 126 ms | 4.7 Hz | – | 79% |
| No BoxerNet, SAM3 uncapped, other user busy 70% | 10.0 Hz, 141 ms | 7.7 Hz | – | 86% |

Every GPU detector held 10 Hz next to the load. SAM3 tracking slowed when it
was uncapped, and held its rate when capped at 5 Hz.

### SAM3

| | Uncapped (`max_tracking_rate_hz: 15`) | Capped at 5 Hz |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tracking rate, one object, alone | 11.8 Hz | 4.85 Hz |
| Camera to result | 150 ms | 146 ms (272 ms before [rammp-perception#8](https://github.com/rammp-org/rammp-perception/pull/8)) |
| GPU busy, SAM3 alone | 48% | 24% |
| Detect, four prompts, next to the detector | 0.7–0.9 s | 0.6–0.8 s |

Before rammp-perception#8, a capped tracking step took a frame first and then
waited out the cap, so it always tracked a frame that was about 120 ms old.

### CPU

Before the fix, the scene segmenter used 5 cores at 10 Hz (YOLOE) or 2.6
(RF-DETR) for about 40 ms of work per frame. The cost was numpy's OpenBLAS:
it starts a worker per core, the workers busy-wait between calls, and the
trackers' small matrix operations wake them every frame. Limiting torch's or
OpenCV's threads, or making CUDA wait without spinning, changed nothing.
`OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1` fixed it
([rammp-perception#6](https://github.com/rammp-org/rammp-perception/pull/6)):

| Scene segmenter CPU at 10 Hz | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YOLOE-26m-seg, GPU | 5.1 cores | 0.49 cores |
| YOLOE-26s-seg, GPU | 5.1 cores | 0.46 cores |
| YOLOE-26s-seg, DLA0 | 4.4 cores | 0.37 cores |
| RF-DETR-Seg S | 2.6 cores | 0.54 cores |

Frame time did not change. Freeing the CPU also sped up the nodes around the
detector: the DLA path went from 8.1 to 9.3 Hz, and BoxerNet from 5.9 to 8.7
Hz. Any module that runs numpy in a loop on the Jetson should set the same
variable.

### snorlax

snorlax computes as fast as abra but delivered fewer messages. In each run
the nodes logged the rates they published at, and the probe counted what
arrived:

| Finalized stack on snorlax | Published (node log) | Received |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YOLOE-26m detections | 9.6–9.9 Hz | 7.9 Hz |
| BoxerNet 3D boxes | 5.0 Hz | 2.9 Hz |
| SAM3 tracking | 4.7–4.8 Hz | 3.8 Hz |

The difference is the kernel's UDP receive buffer. snorlax had Linux's
default of 208 KB, and abra has 32 MB from
`/etc/sysctl.d/60-ros-dds-buffers.conf`. These messages are megabytes each:
a 1280 × 720 frame is 2.7 MB, and a detection with its label image is 1.8 MB.
When the buffer cannot hold all of a message's fragments, DDS drops the whole
message. The loss grows with the traffic: next to an uncapped SAM3 before the
OpenBLAS fix, detections arrived at 5.4 Hz. Set the buffers as in
[Developer setup](/setup#raise-the-dds-socket-buffers) on every Jetson.

Per-frame compute on snorlax matched abra. With the replayed camera,
camera-to-result latency there leaves out the camera driver: 56 ms for
YOLOE-26m against 98 ms on abra with the Gemini.

### Memory

Memory is unified on the Orin, so GPU allocations count against the same
64 GB. On snorlax, whose idle baseline was clean, the finalized stack used
about 15 GB over the idle machine:

| | Over idle |
| --- | --- |
| Scene camera, planner, foxglove, mocks | 2.6 GB |
| + SAM3 loaded and tracking | 11.9 GB |
| + YOLOE-26m and BoxerNet | 15 GB |

abra reported about half that. It was already holding about 18 GB that no
process accounted for when the runs started, and the stack reused it, so its
memory numbers are not used here.

## Repeating the measurements

The bench lives in
[rammp-deployments `perf/`](https://github.com/rammp-org/rammp-deployments/tree/dev/perf).
It builds a manifest from `december_2026/`, brings each configuration up with
sheppy, and writes one JSON file per run. `perf/report.py` turns those files
into the tables on this page. On a Jetson with no camera, `perf/replay_camera.py`
replays a recording in its place.
